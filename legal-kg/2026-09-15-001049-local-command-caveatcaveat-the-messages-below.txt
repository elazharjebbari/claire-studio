  shows that consolidating the taxonomy recovers an equivalent amount of        
  reliability, provided no merge crosses unfairness strata — a legal constraint 
  that purely statistical consolidation systematically violates. Finally, a     
  compact legal language model fine-tuned on the layer approaches the human     
  ceiling and outperforms every judge. We release the annotations, the codebook

  Ce que la recherche établit, affirmation par affirmation

  1. Le terrain — personne ne dit « de quoi parle la clause » sur CLAUDETTE.
  Toute la lignée Lippi et al. 2019 → Drawzeski et al. 2021 → LexGLUE → Galassi 
  et al. 2024 → vague LLM (Panarelli et al., ICAIL 2025 ; Text to Trust, 2025 :
  le fine-tuning complet de BERT reste le meilleur) étiquette l'abusivité seule.
  Le seul corpus contractuel avec des topics est AGB-DE (ACL 2024) : 23 topics
  mais un seul annotateur par clause, aucun coefficient d'accord, et son
  prédécesseur Braun & Matthes 2022 (23 topics, 2 annotateurs, 87 % brut) n'est
  pas publié. Le seul précédent en triple annotation avec κ est OPP-115
  (politiques de confidentialité, Fleiss κ 0,49–0,91).

  2. Le pivot bibliographique — Bayerl & Paul 2011 (méta-analyse, 96 études) :
  le nombre de catégories fait baisser l'accord, mais les auteurs laissent 
  explicitement ouverte la question de savoir si l'effet subsiste sur des
  coefficients corrigés du hasard (κ « a été inventé pour ça »). Votre E2.1
  (+0,067 d'α de 20 → 11 classes, reproduit sur hold-out) répond à cette 
  question — c'est l'angle le plus fort pour la section 2. Marchal et al. COLING
  2022 fournit l'argument que le coût du multi-label doit être mesuré ;
  Passonneau 2006 la distance MASI.

  3. Consolidation sous contrainte juridique. LEDGAR réduit par fréquence,
  GoEmotions par corrélation : aucun précédent de fusion contrainte par le
  signal aval (strates d'abusivité) — à formuler « to our knowledge ». Braun 
  2024 : tous les jeux juridiques effacent le désaccord — votre cascade/soft
  labels y répondent.

  4. Juges LLM. Bavaresco et al. ACL 2025 (JUDGE-BENCH) : pas prêts à remplacer
  les juges humains ; Mukherjee et al. 2026 : le consensus inter-LLM n'est pas 
  l'alignement humain — exactement votre claude↔fable 0,80 vs humain↔LLM ≤ 0,59.
  En droit : Savelka & Ashley 2023, Schepers et al. 2025.

  Title    Executing the Grey List: Interpretable Queries for Unfair-Term       
  Detection in Terms of Service                                                 
  Paper                                                                         
  Author keywords    unfair contract terms                                      
  terms of service                                                              
  directive 93/13                                                               
  legal knowledge representation                                                
  Topics    Computational representations of legal rules and domain-specific    
  languages (DSLs) for law, Explainable AI for legal applications, Information  
  extraction, text understanding (e.g. entailment) from legal data and texts    
  Abstract    The Annex of Directive 93/13/EEC lists the types of contractual   
  terms that may be regarded as unfair: an indicative, non-exhaustive list      
  commonly called the grey list. Automated detectors ignore it and classify     
  sentences with learned models whose decisions cite no legal ground. We turn   
  the grey list into an executable resource. Each clause of a contract,         
  identified by its human-validated theme, is mapped to a simple legal template 
  describing who acts, what is obliged, permitted or forbidden, and under       
  which conditions, deadlines and remedies. Unfairness queries then translate   
  grey-list items into conditions over these templates. They are written        
  without access to the reference labels and fixed before evaluation. Because   
  the list is indicative rather than binding, how well it actually retrieves    
  unfair clauses is an open empirical question, and this is precisely what we   
  measure. On held-out terms of service, we report which grey-list items can be 
  expressed this way, the precision and recall of each query against reference  
  unfairness labels, whether the templates add signal beyond the clause theme   
  alone, and what this interpretability costs compared with a learned text-only 
  model. Legal experts audit the explanations each query produces. False        
  positives are examined, and some point to omissions in the reference corpus   
  itself. Templates, queries and validated structures are released.

  Abstract    The Annex of Directive 93/13/EEC lists the types of contractual   
  terms that may be regarded as unfair: an indicative, non-exhaustive list      
  commonly called the grey list. Automated detectors ignore it and classify     
  sentences with learned models whose decisions cite no legal ground. We turn   
  the grey list into an executable resource. Each clause of a contract,         
  identified by its human-validated theme, is mapped to a simple legal template 
  describing who acts, what is obliged, permitted or forbidden, and under       
  which conditions, deadlines and remedies. Unfairness queries then translate   
  grey-list items into conditions over these templates. They are written        
  without access to the reference labels and fixed before evaluation. Because   
  the list is indicative rather than binding, how well it actually retrieves    
  unfair clauses is an open empirical question, and this is precisely what we   
  measure. On held-out terms of service, we report which grey-list items can be 
  expressed this way, the precision and recall of each query against reference  
  unfairness labels, whether the templates add signal beyond the clause theme   
  alone, and what this interpretability costs compared with a learned text-only 
  model. Legal experts audit the explanations each query produces. False        
  positives are examined, and some point to omissions in the reference corpus   
  itself. Templates, queries and validated structures are released.
