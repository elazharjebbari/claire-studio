The CLAUDETTE/UNFAIR-ToS corpus is the reference benchmark for unfair clauses in online terms of service, but it records only whether a sentence is unfair, never what the clause is about. We add a thematic layer to its 50 contracts: 9,414 sentences, each labelled with one or more of 20 legal themes independently by three trained annotators, and by four large language models on the same sentences. The layer separates two effects that the reliability literature tends to conflate. Letting annotators assign several themes lowers agreement, by much the same amount whatever the number of categories. Merging the taxonomy from 20 themes into 11 recovers an equivalent amount, and the gain holds on contracts held out from the design of the merges. Merging is constrained, however: the taxonomy that agreement alone would select groups clauses that consumer protection law treats differently, at more than twice the cost in downstream signal. A fine-tuned legal encoder outperforms every language-model judge, while remaining clearly below the human ceiling. We release the annotations with their individual votes, the codebook, the protocol and the annotation platform.
