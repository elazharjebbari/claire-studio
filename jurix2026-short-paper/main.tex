\begin{abstract}
The \corpus{}/UNFAIR-ToS corpus is the reference benchmark for detecting unfair
clauses in online terms of service, but it labels unfairness sentence by sentence
and records nothing about what each clause is about. We add a thematic layer to
its 50 contracts: 9,414 sentences, each labelled with one or more of 20 legal
themes independently by three trained annotators, and separately by four large
language models prompted with the same vocabulary on the same sentences. The layer
separates two effects that the reliability literature tends to conflate. The
multi-label format costs 0.073 [0.061, 0.087] of chance-corrected agreement, and
this cost varies by only 0.012 across four granularities; consolidating 20 themes
into 11 recovers 0.067 [0.058, 0.077], a gain that replicates on 17 held-out
contracts. Consolidation is constrained, however: the taxonomy that agreement
alone would select merges across unfairness strata and loses 13.2\% of the
downstream signal. A fine-tuned legal encoder outperforms every judge while
remaining below the human ceiling. We release the annotations, codebook, protocol
and annotation platform.
\end{abstract}
