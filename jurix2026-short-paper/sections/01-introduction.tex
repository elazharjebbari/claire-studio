\corpus{} records whether a sentence in a consumer contract is potentially unfair;
it records nothing about what the clause is about \cite{lippi2019claudette,
lagioia2019deep}. A limitation clause and an arbitration clause are both unfair,
and both are simply unfair. Where clause topics do exist for contracts, the
resources are single-annotated \cite{braun2022topics}, unpublished, or reported
without chance-corrected agreement \cite{loffler2025chilean}, so it is not known
how reliably the task can be performed at all.

We add a thematic layer to the reference benchmark. All 50 contracts and
9,414 sentences of \corpus{} are labelled with one or more of 20 legal themes,
independently by three trained annotators, and separately by four large language
models prompted with the same vocabulary on the same sentences. Individual votes,
the arbitration trail and the resulting gold standard are all released.

The layer settles a question that the reliability literature leaves open. Agreement
is known to fall when annotators may assign several labels, and to rise when
categories are merged \cite{bayerl2011metaanalysis,marchal2022multilabel}; what is
not known is whether these are one effect or two, since they are seldom measured
on the same sentences. Here they are. The multi-label format costs
$0.073$ [0.061, 0.087] of chance-corrected agreement, and this cost varies by only
0.012 across four granularities --- while merging 20 themes into 11 recovers
$0.067$ [0.058, 0.077], a gain that replicates on 17 contracts held out from the
design of the merges. Consolidation is therefore a real lever on reliability, but
not an unconstrained one: the taxonomy that agreement alone would select merges
clauses across unfairness strata, and loses 13.2\% of the downstream unfairness
signal to buy $0.012$ of extra agreement.

We contribute: (i) a thematic layer over \corpus{}, multi-label and triply
annotated, published with its disagreements and with the open-source platform used
to produce it; (ii) a separation of the cost of the multi-label \emph{format} from
the effect of taxonomy \emph{granularity}, measured on the same sentences and
replicated out of sample; and (iii) a legal constraint on consolidation --- merges
must not cross unfairness strata --- together with the price of violating it.
