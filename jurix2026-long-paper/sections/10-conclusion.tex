\section{Conclusion and Availability}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have treated the grey list of Directive 93/13/EEC as what it is---a list of configurations of rights and obligations---and executed it as frozen queries over clause templates extracted automatically from the \corpus{} corpus. The result is a detector whose every decision cites the item it implements, and an item-by-item measurement of how much of the annotated unfairness a literal reading of the Annex retrieves: on held-out contracts, a precise but narrow third, below the clause theme alone and well below a learned encoder. The error analysis shows where literal execution fails and where the benchmark and the Annex part ways. Because the queries are explicit and frozen, the measurement can be repeated on any contract whose clauses carry themes, and revised item by item as the law or its reading changes. The queries with their hashes, the extracted templates, the code and the annotation platform used to build the thematic layer are released.
