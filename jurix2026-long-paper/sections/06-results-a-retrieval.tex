\section{Results A: Expressibility and Per-Item Retrieval}
\label{sec:results}
\textbf{The theme alone is a strong baseline.} On the full corpus, the theme-only predictor reaches an average precision of 0.404 against consensus themes and 0.418 against the arbitrated gold (base rate 0.110), that is, 3.7 times the chance level; the identity of the \emph{combination} of themes carried by a clause reaches 0.589 in document-grouped cross-validation. Any structured method that does not clearly exceed these figures adds explanation but not signal.

\textbf{Expressibility.} Eleven of the seventeen items are structural and expressible without a threshold; two require a declared threshold; one is a procedural proxy; three cannot be expressed from the text of the clause (Table~\ref{tab:greylist}). The items that carry most of the \corpus{} labels---\gi{a}/\gi{b}, \gi{f}/\gi{g}, \gi{j}/\gi{k} and \gi{q}---are all in the structural group.

\textbf{Per-item retrieval.} Table~\ref{tab:queries} reports, for each item scored on the held-out contracts, precision and recall against the mapped reference categories and the difference with the theme-only baseline. The queries are precise and sparse. Precision reaches 0.61 to 0.83 on five of the nine scored items (\gi{b}, \gi{f}, \gi{g}, \gi{j}, \gi{l}), but recall never exceeds 0.38, and it stays at 0.30 on unilateral change, 0.26 on termination and 0.11 on limitation of liability. Over the eight structural items, that is, excluding the procedural proxy \gi{i}, micro-averaged precision is 0.46 and recall 0.15. The pre-registered hypothesis for RQ-A is therefore rejected: the recall threshold of 0.50 is missed on all three target categories. Against the theme alone, the queries are significantly better on one item only, \gi{i}, by $+0.21$ [0.10; 0.30] (Holm-adjusted permutation $p = 0.009$); they are indistinguishable on \gi{g}, \gi{j} and \gi{q}, and significantly worse on \gi{a}, \gi{b}, \gi{f} and \gi{k}.
